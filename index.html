About

Every arrow in the central dogma
is a decision

DNA → mRNA → protein looked like a simple answer when I first met it. It is not. Each step is governed by layers that decide which genes are expressed, when, and how much — and I have spent four years trying to read those layers.

I am completing an MSc in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology at Shahjalal University of Science and Technology, where my thesis asks which transcription factors drive the malignancy of breast cancer subtypes — and where I collect the tumour tissue to test the answer myself. Since 2022 I have worked on seven projects across gene expression and its regulation: mpox, HIV, HPV in head-and-neck cancer, glycoRNA-based detection, crop stress genomics, and now breast cancer.

My research started from a limit. Wet-lab work was expensive and my department's facilities were thin, so I turned to code. Features, labels, cross-validation and hyperparameter tuning were all new to me; I worked through library documentation and ran each method on small expression matrices until I understood what it actually did. Eight months later I ran thirteen machine-learning models on my first mpox dataset — work that became a co-first-authored paper in Scientific Reports. The same frustration, that every new dataset meant rebuilding the pipeline, is why I proposed and built omicML.

Computation took me a long way and then stopped. It can nominate a regulator; it cannot show how that regulator works. So I moved toward the bench: my first PCR, optimised condition by condition until the HPV L1 bands finally came up in tumour tissue. Then a thesis proposal I wrote independently, ethical clearance I drafted and defended before institutional review boards at two hospitals, and now RT-qPCR on specimens I collect myself.

What I keep running into is that regulation is not proportional. Promoters that lose methylation and go down anyway. Transcription factors that rise sharply while their predicted targets do not move at all. Precision in gene regulation lives in some combination of chromatin accessibility, epigenetic modification, transcription-factor binding and alternative splicing — and resolving which one decides takes cell-type-specific chromatin mapping and perturbation. That is the training I am looking for in a PhD.

Host transcriptomic biomarkers of the 2022 Mpox outbreak clade

Does the host respond differently to clade IIb — the lineage behind the 2022 global outbreak — than to the clades that came before it?

Approach

I assembled three public microarray and bulk RNA-seq datasets from GEO covering keratinocytes, dermal fibroblasts, macrophages and colon organoids infected with clade I, clade IIa, clade IIb and Zaire MPXV. After normalisation, PCA/UMAP outlier screening and differential expression with limma and DESeq2, I built protein–protein interaction and cluster networks to isolate hub genes, characterised functional enrichment through GO, KEGG and GSEA, then benchmarked thirteen supervised classifiers in PyCaret and validated the shortlist with the best performers.

What came out

798 differentially expressed genes were unique to clade IIb infection. Intersecting the clade comparisons narrowed these to 13 shared DEGs and finally to six candidate biomarkers — CDC20, CCNB1, CCNA2, BUB1, BUB1B and TXNRD1 — each exceeding 0.7 AUROC under logistic regression and random forest. Five of the six govern cell-cycle progression and the mitotic spindle checkpoint, which suggests clade IIb perturbs host proliferation programmes rather than only the antiviral response one would expect. Candidate drugs were assessed against the signature through DSigDB.

omicML — bioinformatics and machine learning for biomarker discovery, without writing code

Why should every new dataset mean rebuilding the same pipeline — and why should that pipeline be out of reach for anyone without a programming background?

Approach

I proposed the tool to my team and then built the backend: a platform that takes a raw microarray or RNA-seq count matrix end to end in one workflow — preprocessing, imputation, normalisation, outlier removal and batch-effect correction; differential expression (limma for microarray, DESeq2 for RNA-seq) with biomaRt annotation; network and functional enrichment through STRINGdb; and feature selection by RFE-RF followed by predictive modelling in scikit-learn and XGBoost under nested cross-validation. The hard part was the rpy2 layer that lets the R and Python halves talk to each other while keeping package versions compatible.

It also needed a front end, and for that, software engineers. I recruited two batchmates who worked mostly on commercial projects and had never touched research code. As their peer I could not assign them anything, so I scheduled around their timetables and explained the biology underneath — once they understood that the interface was a biomarker-prediction instrument rather than a webpage, they were in.

What came out

In a case study on mpox — a separate analysis from the clade IIb signature above — omicML produced a pan-clade model in which RRAD alone, upregulated in infection with any clade, separated mpox from other viral infections and from healthy specimens at AUROC 0.90.

Regulatory methylation hotspots in HIV infection

Expression analysis tells me which genes change. What regulates the change? Does promoter methylation explain what happens in CD4+ T cells during HIV infection?

Hypothesis

A hypomethylated promoter should raise expression of the gene downstream; a hypermethylated promoter should lower it.

Approach

I integrated Illumina Infinium MethylationEPIC array data with RNA-seq, calling differentially methylated promoters using minfi, limma and missMethyl and linking them to expression changes gene by gene. I then compared six classifiers under nested cross-validation and used Extra Trees feature importance to rank candidates, placing the survivors in context with STRING network and functional enrichment analysis.

What came out

123 genes carried both a methylation and an expression change. 85 of them — 69% — behaved as predicted: 50 upregulated with hypomethylated promoters, 35 downregulated with hypermethylated ones. A seven-gene panel emerged, including LTBP4, STAT1, SNX2, DUOX1, SULF2, TUBGCP2 and SLC2A14, mapping onto TGF-β sequestration and signalling, ROS-mediated killing, microtubule synthesis and glucose transport in infected CD4+ T cells — a coherent picture of how the virus could support its own replication, survival and immune evasion through host gene regulation.

The exceptions were the more interesting result. 21 genes were hypomethylated yet downregulated, and 17 hypermethylated yet upregulated. Methylation failed to explain nearly a third of the set. Something upstream of methylation — chromatin accessibility, transcription-factor occupancy at those promoters — had to be deciding the outcome. That gap is what my MSc thesis was designed around.

Multi-omics inference of cell-type-specific gene regulatory networks in breast cancer

Which transcription factors are dysregulated in ER+/PR+, HER2+ and triple-negative breast cancer relative to normal epithelium — and which regulatory layer decides what their activity actually produces in each cell type?

Computational work

I analysed four public scRNA-seq datasets, resolving malignant populations by clustering and marker-based annotation in Seurat, Scanpy and SingleR. For each subtype I compared differentially expressed genes in malignant versus normal epithelial cells against DEGs from three bulk RNA-seq datasets, and kept only genes supported by both — 141 for ER+/PR+, 62 for HER2+ and 83 for TNBC — because validation runs on whole tissue rather than sorted cells. Curated DoRothEA regulons with logistic regression then prioritised three, four and three candidate transcription factors respectively, each with its target set. ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq and bisulfite-sequencing data are now being layered in to ask whether chromatin state and methylation explain those activities, with probabilistic network modelling to rank core drivers.

At the bench

Histopathology-confirmed tumour and adjacent normal tissue is being collected from 60 patients undergoing surgical resection at Bangladesh Medical University and the National Institute of Cancer Research & Hospital. Tumours are subtyped by immunohistochemistry; I extract total RNA and run RT-qPCR for the candidate TFs and their targets. A treatment arm compares patients after 4, 6 and 8 chemotherapy sessions (10 per group); a recurrence arm compares archived FFPE tissue from 15 recurrent and 15 non-recurrent patients. I drafted the protocol and obtained ethical clearance from the institutional review boards at both hospitals.

Where it stands

In preliminary RT-qPCR, two transcription factors came back significantly upregulated while their predicted target drivers were not differentially expressed at all. Working out why is the current problem — and it is the same shape as the HIV result: a regulatory signal that does not produce the outcome the model predicts. Gene regulation is more labyrinthine than a proportional driver–TF relationship, and the resolution sits in chromatin accessibility, epigenetic modification, TF binding and alternative splicing acting together.

HPV in head-and-neck squamous cell carcinoma across a Bangladeshi cohort

How often does HPV drive head-and-neck cancer in Bangladesh, and which genotypes are circulating? Almost nothing had been published on either.

Approach

416 tissue specimens were screened. I prepared sections by microtomy for histopathological screening, ran real-time PCR for HPV DNA, and used nested PCR and Sanger sequencing to characterise partial sequences of the HPV L1 gene. This was the first PCR I had ever run outside a teaching lab; I optimised the conditions repeatedly until clean L1 bands appeared in tumour samples. Immunohistochemistry was used to assess p16INK4a and pRb expression, and an exploratory machine-learning classification ranked the recorded risk factors.

What came out

Histopathology confirmed HNSCC in 214 of 416 specimens. HPV DNA was detected in 53 of those 214 (24.8%), with HPV16 predominant. In selected HPV-positive tumours, immunohistochemistry showed stronger p16INK4a and weaker pRb staining than healthy controls — consistent with E7-driven Rb inactivation and altered cell-cycle control. In the exploratory classification, HPV status ranked as the strongest predictor, followed by betel quid chewing, which matters in a population where betel quid use is widespread.

glycoRNA signatures for breast, cervical and lung cancer detection

Glycosylated RNAs sit on the cell surface and travel in extracellular vesicles, yet barely any method exists to isolate or quantify them. Can they carry a detectable cancer signal?

Approach

This was a broad programme developing affinity probes for glycoRNA isolation and quantification. My part was the computational arm: differential expression analysis of ncRNA-seq across seven cell lines — ADMSC, BMMSC, HeLa, MCF7, TM6, A549 and H1975 — covering breast, cervical and lung models. I profiled glycoRNA expression both on the cell surface and in extracellular vesicles, annotated against RNAcentral, filtered sncRNAs, and selected features by RFE-RF before benchmarking classifiers.

What came out

Y RNAs and U RNAs emerged as the most promising classifying signatures across all three cancer types, and a subset of glycoRNAs tracked with epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition — linking them not just to detection but to the step where tumours become invasive.

RNA modification and stress response in crop genomes

Which genes write, erase and read the RNA modification m⁶A in crop genomes — and how does that machinery respond when the plant is under stress?

Approach

A genome-wide survey of soybean for m⁶A regulators, with MUSCLE alignment of regulatory protein sequences across 13 species for phylogenetic analysis, expression profiling in root and nodule from normalised FPKM data, microRNA interaction prediction, and response profiling under environmental stress and soybean mosaic virus infection. In parallel, on tomato under salt stress: 11 RNA-seq and 7 microarray datasets condensed into a six-gene predictive classifier, then validated at the bench — RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, gel electrophoresis and qRT-PCR with ΔΔCt quantification.

What came out

42 candidate m⁶A regulators in soybean: 12 writers, 11 erasers and 19 readers. GmECT9 was highly expressed across root and nodule samples, and under combined heat and water deficit GmMTB2 rose while GmALKBH9B2 fell — a divergence between writer and eraser that points at active remodelling of the m⁶A landscape under stress rather than a passive shutdown.
